Study and work in the UK

Earn experience while you study, then work full-time after you graduate

Here is how work rights usually apply on a UK Student visa, in simple terms.

During your course

Term time

Up to 20 hours a week

  • For students studying at degree level.
  • Courses below degree level are usually limited to 10 hours a week.
  • Part-time work only, alongside your studies.
Official university breaks

Vacations

Full-time work allowed

  • Only during your university's official vacation periods.
  • For many one-year master's courses, the summer dissertation period counts as term time.
  • Always confirm your own term dates with your university.
Graduate Route

After graduation

Full-time, most jobs

  • 2 years if you apply by 31 December 2026.
  • 18 months if you apply from 1 January 2027.
  • 3 years for PhD and other doctoral graduates.
  • You must be eligible and apply before your Student visa expires.
The week runs Monday to SundayYour limit covers all your jobs added together. You cannot average hours across weeks.
Unpaid work countsUnpaid work is included in your weekly hours, not just paid shifts.
No self-employment or freelancingOn a Student visa you cannot be self-employed, freelance or run a business. This includes many gig-economy apps.

Please note: Work rights depend on your visa conditions and university term dates. Always check your eVisa and get advice before accepting work. Immigration rules can change.

FAQ

Your questions, answered

Am I eligible to study in the UK?

Eligibility depends on the course. Universities look at your academic results, English language ability and, for some courses, work experience or a portfolio. Share your profile and we will tell you which courses you can realistically apply for.

How much does it cost?

Tuition fees vary by university, course and level, and you also need to budget for living costs, the visa fee and the Immigration Health Surcharge. We give you a clear cost estimate for each shortlisted university before you apply.

Can I get a scholarship?

Many UK universities offer merit-based and country-specific scholarships, usually as a reduction in tuition fees. Awards are competitive and not guaranteed. We help you identify the ones you qualify for and apply on time.

Do I need IELTS?

Most courses need proof of English. IELTS is widely accepted, and some universities accept other tests or, in certain cases, your previous English-medium results. Requirements differ by university, so we check them for your shortlist.

How does the Student visa process work?

Once you accept an offer and meet its conditions, the university issues a Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS). You then apply online for a Student visa and show you meet the financial and other requirements. We guide you through each step; visa decisions are made only by UK Visas and Immigration.

Can I work while studying?

Students on a Student visa studying at degree level can usually work up to 20 hours per week during term time and full-time during official university vacations. Self-employment and freelancing are not allowed. See Study and work in the UK for details.

Can I stay and work in the UK after I graduate?

Eligible graduates can apply for the Graduate Route. It currently lasts 2 years for applications made by 31 December 2026 and 18 months for applications made from 1 January 2027, with 3 years for PhD graduates. Rules can change, so always check the latest official guidance.

Does IFS Consultants charge for counselling?

Your initial counselling session is free. We explain any service charges clearly before you decide to proceed.
